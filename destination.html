About Ketchikan

Ketchikan is a port city in the Alaska Panhandle, built over the waters of the Inside Passage and surrounded by the Tongass National Forest — the largest temperate rainforest in the world. Known as the "Salmon Capital of the World," Ketchikan receives more annual rainfall than nearly any other city in the United States.

The city is famous for its collection of totem poles, Creek Street (a boardwalk over Ketchikan Creek where brothels once operated during the Gold Rush), and its vibrant Tlingit, Haida, and Tsimshian cultural heritage.

Ships dock directly at the downtown pier, making it one of the most walkable ports of call in all of Alaska. The city centre is compact and easily explored on foot.

Top Attractions

Creek Street

Historic boardwalk with boutiques, galleries, and Dolly's House Museum.

Totem Bight State Park

A collection of authentic and recreated totem poles with a clan house.

Misty Fjords National Monument

Accessible by floatplane or boat. Dramatic granite cliffs and pristine wilderness.

Ketchikan Creek Salmon Run

Watch salmon fight upstream July–September. Bears sometimes join the show.
